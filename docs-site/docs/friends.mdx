# Playing with friends

Two ways, depending on where your friends are.

## On your own network

1. **Settings → Multiplayer**, turn on **Allow LAN connections**.
2. **Restart server** — the engine reads that setting when it starts.
3. Copy **Your link — on this network** and send it over.

They paste it into the app's *Join a friend* box, or open it in a browser.
Joining runs nothing locally, so it starts in seconds.

## Over the internet

The app opens a Cloudflare tunnel from your machine outward. **No ports are
forwarded and nothing on your network is exposed** — the tunnel carries only the
game, and only to people holding a current invitation.

1. **Settings → Multiplayer**, turn on **Set up sharing over the internet**.
   This only reveals the controls; it changes no hosting setting on its own.
2. **Share with friends.** The first time, it secures the server's internal
   credentials — it backs up the database first and keeps every account and
   character. It also restarts the game services once, and unticks **Allow LAN
   connections**: while sharing, the invitation is the only way in, so the game
   ports stop listening on your network.
3. Click **Your link — over the internet** to copy the whole invitation.

Your friend opens it, and either logs in with a game account, creates one from
the invitation page, or registers at the login screen with `_M`/`_F`.

Keep the app open and the computer awake while people are playing.

### Temporary links change every time

Without a Cloudflare account you get a **Quick Tunnel**, and Cloudflare hands
out a new `trycloudflare.com` address on every start. A crash, a restart or a
Repair therefore means sending a new link.

Quick Tunnels are meant for testing: no uptime guarantee, and at most **200
requests in flight at once**. Busy asset loading can reach that.

### A link that stays the same

Connect a Cloudflare account and use your own domain. You need a free account, a
domain on it, and an **API token** with:

| Permission | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Zone → Zone → Read** | your domain |
| **Zone → DNS → Edit** | your domain |
| **Account → Cloudflare Tunnel → Edit** | the account owning it |

Create it under **My Profile → API Tokens → Create Token → Create Custom
Token**. It is used only during setup; the tunnel credential then lives in your
operating system's secure password store.

Pick a hostname you are **not** already using — setup refuses to overwrite an
existing DNS record rather than replacing a site you have.

With a named tunnel the address is stable, and the invitation is kept across
restarts, so the whole link keeps working until you replace it.

## Controlling access

**Create a new link** replaces the invitation. The old link stops working
immediately and anyone using it is disconnected — that is what to reach for if a
link ends up somewhere public.

**Stop sharing** does the same and closes the tunnel, without stopping your own
game.

**Invitation lasts** sets an expiry in days, or 0 for none. Replacing is the
real control; expiry is a weaker version of it.

## What friends can and cannot do

They get an ordinary player account. They do not get GM commands unless you have
installed a mod that grants commands to everyone — **player-commands** does
exactly that, and it applies to invited friends too.

Failed logins are rate-limited per account and per browser, so one friend
mistyping a password cannot lock out the group.

## If it will not start

The reason appears in red under the buttons. `sharing.log` in the data folder
(**Settings → Save data → Open data folder**) has the full story, including the
Cloudflare helper's download and cloudflared's own output, and **Copy
diagnostics** includes its tail.
