# Settings: Population

A world of one can feel like a world of one. The population engine fills towns
and fields with characters who walk around, fight, sit in Prontera and open
stalls.

They appear as ordinary players, so friends who join see the same crowd and
companions you do.

## Enable

Off by default. With it off the databases are never loaded and its timers never
run, so a disabled engine costs nothing.

## Density

How crowded a map is, as a percentage of what the server's spawn tables ask
for. The world keeps the shape it was authored with; only the number of
characters on it changes.

## Limit

A ceiling across every map at once, not a target. Spawning is demand-driven —
only maps somebody is on get built, and a map holds twenty to forty by the
spawn tables — so this only binds if a group fans out across dozens of maps at
once. In a solo game it never comes into play.

## AI companions

Any fake player can become a temporary party companion. Create a party, whisper
`party`, `pt`, `join`, or `invite` to the character, and send a normal party
invitation within 60 seconds. It accepts automatically. A party can have up to
four companions.

Companions follow their recruiter across maps, catch up by teleporting when
necessary, and take separate positions around the player while idle. Removing
one from the party releases it. Companions and their assigned behaviour do not
persist across a server or app restart.

### Party-chat controls

Only the current party leader can control companions, and commands work only in
party chat. Matching is case-insensitive.

| Mode | Role in combat |
|---|---|
| `attack` or a message containing that word | All companions independently engage monsters near their owner. |
| `defensive` or a message containing that word | Companions attack the owner's target and defend party members. This is the default. |
| `passive` or a message containing that word | Companions ignore monsters but continue following and using support skills. |

The quick versions are `atk`, `def`, and `pass`. To prevent accidental orders
while discussing character stats, a quick command must be the entire message.

Assign a role to one companion with its name and a role word, in either order:

```text
Mirarir attacker
Galashiel supp
Hanaban tk
```

Tank (`tank`, `tk`) prioritises threats to the party. Support (`support`,
`supp`) moves toward injured allies and uses ally skills. Attacker (`attacker`,
`dd`) focuses on offense and skips ally-targeted skills. The companion confirms
the change in party chat. Roles guide skills the class already has; they do not
turn a class without healing skills into a healer.

### Death and resurrection

A dead companion remains in the party and can be targeted while its owner stays
on the same map. Priest-line companions automatically use level 3 Resurrection
on dead players or companions, restoring 50% HP. Their Blue Gemstone supply is
virtual and unlimited because shells have no accessible inventory. Players can
also use a Yggdrasil Leaf on a dead companion.

When a companion dies it says so in party chat, with the map and cell where it
fell, and the Companions window marks it ✝ until it is revived.

Leaving the map while a companion is dead releases it and removes it from the
party.

### Companion roaming

Off by default. On, the Companions window offers **Roam**: on each companion's
row of the Party tab, and for all of them under *Movement* on the Battle tab. A
roaming companion walks the map on its own, the way the fake players in the
fields do, instead of following you. It fights by its stance around itself,
stays in your party (EXP and drops still reach you), comes along when you change
maps, and comes back to you with **Recall**. Off, every companion follows and
nothing in game mentions roaming; a companion saved as roaming follows until the
setting is on again.

## What it costs

Roughly 70–80 KB of server memory per character, and they only think while a
real player is near enough to see them. The Settings page estimates the memory
for the number you have chosen.

## If the server becomes unstable

Turn it off and see whether the problem follows. It is the most complex thing
in the server and the easiest to rule out, and doing so costs you nothing
permanent — the world is unchanged underneath.

If a crash happens with the engine **off**, that is a different fault and worth
reporting with **Copy diagnostics**, which now carries preserved crash reports.
